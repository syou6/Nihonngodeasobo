The 4 Japanese Pitch Accent Patterns

Every Japanese word follows one of four pitch patterns. The whole system comes down to one question: where does the pitch drop? That drop point is called the accent nucleus (核). Here are all four, with the high (高) and low (低) moras shown.

1. Heiban (平板) — no drop

Pitch starts low on mora 1, rises, and stays high to the end (including the particle). No nucleus. This is the most common pattern.

にほんが 日本語 — nihongo (Japanese)

2. Atamadaka (頭高) — drop after mora 1

Mora 1 is high, then the pitch drops immediately and stays low. Nucleus = mora 1.

めがね 眼鏡 — megane (glasses)

3. Nakadaka (中高) — drop in the middle

Pitch rises, stays high, then drops somewhere in the middle of the word. Nucleus = a middle mora.

たまご 卵 — tamago (egg)

4. Odaka (尾高) — drop after the last mora

The whole word is high after mora 1, then the pitch drops on the following particle. Nucleus = the last mora. In isolation it sounds like heiban — you only hear the difference when a particle follows.

おとうとが 弟が — otōto-ga (younger brother + が)
